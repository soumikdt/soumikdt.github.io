Overview

A central challenge in topological data analysis (TDA) and kernel-based methods is high ambient dimensionality: both persistent homology and kernel pipelines become computationally intractable as the input dimension increases. My work looks at ways to make these computations smaller while provably keeping the structure they are meant to capture, along three lines: simplifying random simplicial complexes by collapses, low-dimensional embeddings for Gaussian kernels on manifolds, and approximation algorithms in computational geometry.

Collapses of random simplicial complexes

The strong collapse of a simplicial complex, proposed by Barmak and Minian, is a combinatorial collapse of a complex onto a sub-complex. It has received attention in computational topology because of its empirically observed usefulness in reducing the size of simplicial complexes while preserving the homotopy class.

We consider the strong collapse process on random simplicial complexes. For the Erdős–Rényi random clique complex X(n, c/n) on n vertices with edge probability c/n, c > 1, we show that after any maximal sequence of strong collapses the remaining subcomplex, or core, has

(1 − γ)(1 − cγ)·n + o(n) vertices asymptotically almost surely,

where γ is the least non-negative fixed point of f(x) = exp(−c(1 − x)) in (0, 1).

Related publication: J-D. Boissonnat, K. Dutta, S. Dutta, S. Pritam, On Strong Edge Collapse of Random Simplicial Complexes, SoCG 2024 · full version

Low-dimensional embeddings for Gaussian kernels on manifolds

The Gaussian kernel is a widely used similarity measure that captures nonlinear relationships in data, and it gives rise to the Gaussian kernel distance, with applications in kernel PCA, spectral clustering and more. Computing these distances between many pairs of points can be computationally prohibitive.

Using the Random Fourier Features (RFF) method of Rahimi and Recht, Chen and Phillips proved that for points in a ball of radius R, a number of random features depending on R suffices to preserve all pairwise Gaussian kernel distances up to a (1 ± ε) factor. For data on an arbitrary submanifold ℳ ⊂ ℝN with positive reach and intrinsic dimension d, we establish the first uniform, relative-error embedding theorem for Gaussian kernel distances: roughly

O( (d² / ε²) · (log N + log (1/εδ)) ) random Fourier features

are enough to guarantee, with probability 1 − δ, that the kernel distance between every pair of points on the manifold is preserved up to relative error ε. The bound depends only logarithmically on the ambient dimension and on manifold parameters such as volume and reach, and needs no bound on the ambient radius.

Persistent homology is preserved

Under the same RFF embedding, the persistent homology of the manifold is preserved: the weighted Čech and Rips filtrations built with the Gaussian Kernel Power Distance (GKPD) are (1 ± ε⋆)-interleaved, where ε⋆ incorporates both the distance distortion and the approximation of the kernel weights. This extends the point-cloud interleaving result of Boissonnat and Dutta to the continuous manifold setting.

Algorithmic consequences

  • Kernel k-means — Euclidean k-means costs on the embedded points (1 ± 2ε)-approximate the true RKHS clustering cost.
  • Kernel distance matching — the same cost-transfer argument applies to shape and point-cloud matching under Gaussian kernel-distance costs.
  • Kernel nearest-neighbour search — Euclidean nearest-neighbour search on the embedded points gives a (1 + O(ε))-approximate nearest neighbour under the kernel distance.
  • Kernel SVM — the embedding preserves the large-margin geometry up to (1 ± O(ε)), so a kernel SVM can be replaced by a linear SVM trained on O(nt) random features instead of an O(n²) Gram matrix.

Empirical validation

A numerical experiment on a family of “slow helix” manifolds — one-dimensional curves that nonetheless use all N ambient coordinates — measures the smallest RFF dimension needed to keep the 95th-percentile relative kernel-distance error below 1%. The measured threshold does not grow linearly with the ambient dimension, supporting the intrinsic-dimension-only scaling of the theorem.

Paper: S. Dutta, K. Dutta, Low-Dimensional Embeddings for Gaussian Kernels on Manifolds, submitted to SODA 2027 · arXiv:2609.15179

Approximate colorful Carathéodory

The colorful Carathéodory theorem (Bárány, 1982) guarantees that, given d + 1 colour classes in ℝd each containing the origin in their convex hull, one can choose one point per colour whose convex hull also contains the origin. Computing such a colourful simplex requires repeated Euclidean projections onto polytopes, which is expensive in high dimension.

We combine the iterative colour-pivoting framework of Bárány and Onn (1997) with the nearly-linear-time mirror-descent projection method of Mirrokni et al. (2015), and design a compatible approximate projection subroutine that always returns a point compatible with the pivot step while remaining accurate to within an additive ε₁ of the true projection.

We prove that the scheme converges: each pivot strictly decreases the distance from the origin to the hull of the current simplex, the target tolerance ε₂ is reached after O(1/ε₂²) pivots with total running time O(d³/ε₂⁸), and the guarantees survive when the input points are known only up to small numerical or measurement errors.

Paper: Compatible Projection Methods for Approximate Colorful Carathéodory, in preparation; target venue SoCG 2027.
