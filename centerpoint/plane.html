What this shows, and why the note adds the lift and the weights

  • Forster spreads directions. Run it on the narrow cone: the directions fan out and both eigenvalues reach 1. This is the spread premise, S ⪰ γI.
  • It keeps every sign. A is linear, so lines through the origin stay lines through the origin. The count in the halfspace is identical on both panels.
  • Even is not uniform. Isotropy, (1/n) Σ uiuiᵀ = I/2, means the doubled angles 2φi balance (the inset's centroid reaches 0). The directions themselves can still sit in a fan over half the circle.
  • It ignores ±. u and −u add the same uuᵀ, so isotropy says nothing about balance. The mean direction usually stays far from zero; on the narrow cone, all directions remain on one side of a line. Spread without balance does not force points onto both sides.
  • It only sees the origin. Without the lift, the only halfspaces it can talk about pass through the fixed origin. Lifting to (pi, 1) turns every halfspace through any candidate centre into a linear one, and the 1/qi-weighted mean c is the centre that makes the directions balance.
  • It can fail. On the crowded line, more than half the vectors lie on one line through the origin. No transform exists, the iteration stalls, and the Forster decomposition would keep that line as its subspace instead.
This page uses exact unit normalization in floating point, with no rational normalizers qi. The iteration is A ← M−1/2A with M = (2/n) Σ uiuiᵀ, the simple fixed-point scheme related to Tyler's M-estimator. It is not the strongly polynomial algorithm of Diakonikolas–Tzamos–Kane.
