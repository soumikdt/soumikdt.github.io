Proposition 6 · the proof, one move at a time

Why the weighted mean is deep

The proof follows 24 sample points as they are lifted, transformed, normalized and projected. In the end the projected values force many points into every halfspace through c.

Order of computation in the algorithm
Lift vᵢ = (pᵢ, 1)→ Forster map A→ Normalizers qᵢ ≈ ‖Avᵢ‖→ yᵢ = Avᵢ/qᵢ, check S ⪰ γI→ c = Σpᵢ/qᵢ ÷ Σ1/qᵢ
Used only in the proof, for one halfspace at a time
Direction a: halfspace aᵀ(x − c) ≥ 0→ h = (a, −aᵀc): the same halfspace, lifted→ Normal t = A⁻ᵀh after the map A→ Projection zᵢ = ⟨t̂, yᵢ⟩ ∈ [−1, 1]→ Balance Σzᵢ = 0 ⇒ count ≥ mγ/2

c is computed last, from A and the qᵢ. The proof below starts from c and then shows why it is deep, so the highlighted chip tells you which quantity each step is about.

Step 1 of 7

A
Forster map on the lifted vectors (3 × 3). It spreads their directions evenly. Its scale does not matter.
qᵢ
From the doubling loop: ‖Avᵢ‖ ≤ qᵢ ≤ 2‖Avᵢ‖. Far-out points get large qᵢ.
c
Average of the original pᵢ with weights 1/qᵢ.
h
The halfspace in lifted coordinates: hᵀvᵢ = aᵀpᵢ − aᵀc = ℓ(pᵢ). It is the normal of the plane through the origin.
zᵢ
Projection of each yᵢ onto the unit normal t̂. Its sign says which side pᵢ is on.
#
Points inside this halfspace, compared with the bound.
data point in the halfspace (zᵢ ≥ 0) outside (zᵢ < 0) c and its images
The points are recentred and rescaled for display; this does not change depth. A comes from a Forster iteration and is taken symmetric, so the morph from the identity has no spurious rotation. The halfspace shown is the worst direction for c, where the fewest points are inside. The count uses γ = λmin(S), the sharpest γ this sample allows; the algorithm only certifies γ = 1/(8r).
