Low-Dimensional Embeddings for Gaussian Kernels on Manifolds

Soumik Dutta, Kunal Dutta · Submitted to SODA 2027

Random Fourier features turn the Gaussian kernel into an ordinary Euclidean inner product, at the price of a random error. For data on a submanifold of ℝN with positive reach and intrinsic dimension d, we prove the first uniform, relative-error embedding theorem for Gaussian kernel distances: roughly O((d²/ε²) · (log N + log(1/εδ))) features preserve the distance between every pair of points up to a factor (1 ± ε). The bound depends only logarithmically on the ambient dimension and needs no bound on the ambient radius. The same embedding preserves persistent homology, and carries over to kernel k-means, nearest-neighbour search and SVMs.

Between-group interactions may delay a social tipping point in sustainable diets

Soumik Dutta, Coralie Kerluc, Karolina Safarzyńska · 2026 · Submitted to Climatic Change

We developed and implemented an agent-based model in Python to simulate social imitation in the food-consumption behaviour of more than 80,000 European households, and the CO₂ emissions that follow from it. The work covered data preprocessing, social network generation and Monte Carlo experiments, and was carried out at the University of Warsaw in 2024 – 2025.

The paper is under review and not yet public; details will be added here once it is.

On Edge Collapse of Random Simplicial Complexes

Jean-Daniel Boissonnat, Kunal Dutta, Soumik Dutta, Siddharth Pritam · SoCG 2024

Edge collapse shrinks a simplicial complex without changing its homotopy type, which makes persistent homology cheaper to compute. We study how much it saves on the Erdős–Rényi random clique complex X(n, c/√n), and give upper and lower bounds on the number of edges left after any number of collapsing phases, as well as on the final core. The proof combines local weak convergence with a new concentration inequality for typically Lipschitz functions on random graphs. These are the first theoretical results proved for edge collapses on random (or non-random) simplicial complexes.
